Our friendship

How we got here

Gage and I met when we both worked at Ashley Furniture. I was a dispatcher, and he was a technician. We started making small talk in between orders and realized we liked a lot of the same things.

Eventually, we traded contact information and started hanging out after work. We have known each other for six years now and still talk almost every week.

How I communicate

I am usually energetic and loud. I talk a lot of smack to Gage, but it is in a brotherly way. I am joking around with him, and there is no serious insult behind it.

How Gage communicates

Gage gives the smack talk right back. He has that same brotherly sense of humor, but he is also understanding and pretty chill. We share the humor while bringing different energy to the conversation.

Communication in real life

Three concepts in our friendship

01

Selective attention

Textbook section: “Selective Attention”

What it means

The textbook describes selective attention as “concentrating on one part of the environment.” In other words, we focus on certain information while other details fade into the background.

How it connects to us

When Gage and I first talked between orders, our shared interests stood out to me. Those details gave me a reason to keep talking with him beyond what our jobs required. Looking back, this is an example of how what I pay attention to can shape my impression of someone and where a conversation goes.

02

Interpretation

Textbook section: “Interpretation”

What it means

The textbook describes interpretation as “giving meaning to information.” Context helps us decide what a message means, especially when it could be understood in more than one way.

How it connects to us

Our smack talk is a good example. Someone who did not know us might hear an insult, but we understand it as joking between friends. Six years of knowing each other gives those comments a different meaning. That also reminds me that a joke depends on how it is received, so I should not assume every person would take it the same way Gage does.

03

Self-concept

Textbook section: “Self-Concept”

What it means

The textbook describes self-concept as our “general perception of who we are.” It includes how we describe ourselves, the roles we fill, and how we evaluate ourselves.

How it connects to us

I describe myself as energetic and loud, which is part of my self-image. That shows up in the way I joke and bring energy to our conversations. I see Gage as more relaxed and understanding, although that is my perception of him, not necessarily his own self-concept. Recognizing the difference helps me see how my view of myself and my view of him both affect how I communicate.

What I learned

We do not have to be exactly alike.

I learned that there is a good balance between what Gage and I have in common and the ways we are different. Our shared interests and sense of humor give us something to connect over. My louder energy and his more relaxed personality give our conversations a balance that works for us.

This project helped me put words to that. What I notice about Gage, how I interpret our jokes, and how I see myself all play a part in our communication. I think that mix helps explain why we have stayed friends for six years and still talk almost every week.

Textbook source

The Communication Age: Connecting and Engaging, Chapter 2, “Perception, Self, and Communication.” Short quotations come from the sections named above. The friendship examples and reflection connect those concepts to my experience with Gage.
